How It Works

Releasing Tight Fascia & Trapped Nerve Branches

In chronic myofascial pain syndrome — for example, following a whiplash injury — the fascia — the connective tissue that surrounds and separates muscles — can become thickened, restricted, and adhered, often entrapping the small nerve branches that run through it. This can drive persistent, deep, aching pain in the trapezius, upper back, and lower back that doesn't respond well to stretching, massage, or physical therapy alone.

Fascia hydrodissection uses precisely placed fluid, guided in real time by ultrasound, to mechanically separate and loosen these restricted fascial planes and free the nerve branches running through them.

The two-step protocol
1 Ultrasound-guided hydrodissection using D5 (5% dextrose) and a local anesthetic — this first procedure is itself diagnostic, showing exactly how tight and restricted the fascia is, and often provides meaningful relief on its own
2 If relief is incomplete, hyaluronidase is added on a second, ultrasound-guided procedure to more fully break down thickened, fibrotic fascia and release entrapped nerve branches

Common Questions

What is fascia hydrodissection? +

It's an ultrasound-guided injection procedure that uses fluid — and, when needed, the enzyme hyaluronidase — to mechanically loosen thickened, restricted fascia and free the small nerve branches that run through it. It's used for chronic myofascial pain syndrome affecting the trapezius, upper back, and lower back — including cases that develop after a whiplash injury.

Why is the first procedure done without hyaluronidase? +

The first procedure uses D5 (5% dextrose) and a local anesthetic. It's diagnostic — it shows exactly how tight and restricted the fascia is — and it often provides meaningful relief by itself. Hyaluronidase is only added on a second procedure if that relief is incomplete.

What is hyaluronidase and is it safe? +

Hyaluronidase is an enzyme that temporarily breaks down hyaluronic acid within tissue, helping to loosen thick, fibrotic fascia and release adhered nerve branches. It's a well-studied medication with an established safety profile and a low risk of complications when used in this way, under ultrasound guidance.

How many procedures will I need? +

Usually 2–3 procedures total are sufficient. Some patients get lasting relief from the first, diagnostic procedure alone; others benefit from adding hyaluronidase on a second procedure. We'll build a specific plan with you based on your response to treatment.

Why does ultrasound guidance matter? +

Blind injections frequently miss their target. Every fascia hydrodissection procedure at OCMSK is performed under real-time ultrasound guidance, so the fluid is delivered precisely to the restricted fascial planes and nerve branches causing your pain.
