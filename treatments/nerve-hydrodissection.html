How It Works

Freeing a Nerve From Surrounding Tissue

Peripheral nerves can become compressed or entrapped by surrounding scar tissue, thickened fascia, or narrow anatomical tunnels — as in carpal tunnel syndrome at the wrist, cubital tunnel syndrome at the elbow, tarsal tunnel syndrome at the ankle, and meralgia paresthetica at the thigh. This entrapment restricts the nerve's normal gliding motion, driving pain, numbness, tingling, and weakness in the area it supplies.

Nerve hydrodissection uses precisely placed fluid, guided in real time by ultrasound, to mechanically separate the nerve from the tissue restricting it — relieving pressure and restoring its normal mobility.

The nerve hydrodissection process
1 Ultrasound identifies the exact site and severity of the nerve compression
2 Fluid — D5 (5% dextrose) and/or a local anesthetic — is injected precisely around the nerve, separating it from surrounding scar tissue or fascia
3 The nerve is guided through its full range of motion under ultrasound to confirm the entrapment has been released
4 For select nerve injuries, platelet-rich plasma (PRP) may be added to further support nerve healing

Cubital Tunnel Syndrome ›

Tarsal Tunnel Syndrome ›

Compression of the posterior tibial nerve at the ankle, causing foot pain, numbness, and tingling.

Compression of the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve, causing numbness and burning on the outer thigh.

Common Questions

What is nerve hydrodissection? +

It's a precise, ultrasound-guided injection that separates an entrapped nerve from surrounding scar tissue or fascia using an injected fluid, restoring its normal gliding motion and relieving pressure.

What conditions is it used for? +

Most commonly carpal tunnel syndrome, cubital tunnel syndrome, tarsal tunnel syndrome, and meralgia paresthetica — as well as other peripheral nerve compression or entrapment syndromes.

Can this help me avoid surgery? +

Many cases of nerve compression, such as carpal tunnel or cubital tunnel syndrome, respond well to ultrasound-guided hydrodissection and neuromodulation, helping patients avoid or delay a surgical nerve release. Response varies by severity — we'll review your ultrasound findings at your consultation.

Is PRP ever combined with nerve hydrodissection? +

For select nerve injuries, we add platelet-rich plasma to the hydrodissection to further support nerve healing. We'll let you know if this is a good fit for your case.

Why does ultrasound guidance matter? +

Blind injections frequently miss their target. Every procedure at OCMSK is performed under real-time ultrasound guidance, so the fluid is delivered to the exact site of nerve entrapment.

Is this covered by insurance? +

Yes. With an accepted plan (Cigna, Anthem Blue Cross, Aetna, United Healthcare, or Medicare PPO), nerve hydrodissection is billed to your insurance at $0 out-of-pocket cost to you. Self-pay patients are welcome with transparent, accessible rates — see our pricing page for details.

What happens at the first visit? +

A full history and physical exam plus a diagnostic ultrasound of the affected nerve — so you leave with an accurate diagnosis, an understanding of every factor contributing to it, and a clear treatment plan.
